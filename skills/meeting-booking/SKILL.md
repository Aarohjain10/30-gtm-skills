# Meeting Booking

## What this gets you
A clear path from "sounds interesting" to a confirmed time on both calendars, with reminders, a plan for reschedules and no-shows, and a handoff note so the person taking the call is prepared.

## Before you start
Read `business-profile.md` if it exists. If not, ask once, max 6 questions:
1. Who takes the call and what time zone are they in?
2. Which days and hours can they take calls? Any buffer rules?
3. Call length and format (phone, video link)?
4. Do you use a booking link? If yes, paste it. If you have a live calendar tool connected, use it to read free slots; otherwise ask for 4 to 6 open times.
5. What did the prospect say, and which channel did they use?
6. What should the prospect do to prepare, if anything?

## Steps
1. Confirm the reply is real interest (use `reply-triage` if unsure). Only offer a meeting to someone who asked for one or showed interest.
2. Match the channel. Reply where they replied. An email reply gets an email. Do not jump to a call or text unless they gave a number and agreed to it, and then US mobile numbers only and only with the consent your market requires.
3. Write the booking message: thank them in one line, state the purpose and length, give two or three specific time options with the time zone written in their zone, and add a booking link as an alternative. Ask for a reply with a number or a better time.
4. Handle time zones: write times in the prospect's zone and, once, in yours. Never assume. If their zone is unknown, ask or infer from their signature and say your assumption aloud.
5. When they pick, confirm in one message: date, time, zone, length, how to join, who joins from your side, and one line on what you will cover. Send the calendar invite.
6. Set reminders (starting settings, tune to your data): one the day before, one about 1 hour before, each short and with the join link.
7. Reschedule: say yes without friction, offer new times, keep the same thread. Do not count a reschedule as a failure.
8. No-show: wait 10 minutes, then send one friendly note within the hour with two new times. If there is no reply, one more attempt after 2 business days, then stop and mark as lost for now.
9. Write the handoff note for whoever takes the call (format below).

## Output format
1. Booking message (DRAFT).
2. Confirmation message and calendar invite text.
3. Reminder messages (day before, 1 hour before).
4. Reschedule and no-show messages.
5. Handoff note: Who, company, how they came in, what they said (quote), what we know, what they asked for, open questions, suggested opener, risks.
6. Status: where this lead sits (Interested, Proposed times, Booked, Held, No-show, Lost for now).

## Worked example
Example scenario, not a customer. A fictional payroll consultancy, Ledgerline Advisory, emailed Dana Reyes, operations manager at a 30 person landscaping company in Denver (Mountain Time). Ledgerline's consultant is Chris, in New York (Eastern Time), taking 20 minute video calls, 10:00 to 16:00 Eastern.

Dana's reply: "Interesting. Payroll has been a headache since we hired seasonal crews. Can we talk this week?"

**Booking message (DRAFT, awaiting approval):**
Subject: Re: payroll for seasonal crews
"Thanks Dana. Seasonal crews do make payroll messy. Chris, our consultant, can do a 20 minute video call. These work this week (your time, Mountain):
Wednesday 9:00 am
Thursday 8:30 am
Thursday 11:00 am
If none fit, tell me a window that does, or pick any time here: [REAL BOOKING LINK]. Anything you want us to look at beforehand, such as how crews are paid now?"

**Dana replies:** "Thursday 8:30 works."

**Confirmation:**
"Booked: Thursday, Nov 12, 8:30 am Mountain (10:30 am Eastern), 20 minutes, video. Link: [REAL VIDEO LINK]. Chris will join. We will talk about how seasonal crews are paid today and where it gets painful. Invite on the way."

**Reminders (starting setting):** Wednesday: "Reminder: tomorrow 8:30 am Mountain with Chris, link inside. Reply here if anything changes." Thursday 7:30 am: "See you at 8:30. Link: [REAL VIDEO LINK]."

**Dana asks to move:** "Sorry, can we do Friday?" Reply: "Of course. Friday at 9:00 or 10:30 am Mountain?" Update the invite and reminders.

**No-show path:** At 8:40 with no one on the call, wait until 8:45, then: "Hi Dana, we missed each other this morning, no problem. Want to try Friday 9:00 or 10:30 am Mountain, or send a better time?" If silent after 2 business days, one last line, then mark Lost for now.

**Handoff note for Chris:**
Who: Dana Reyes, operations manager, 30 person landscaping company, Denver.
How in: replied to our first email.
Quote: "Payroll has been a headache since we hired seasonal crews."
We know: seasonal hiring, small team. We do not know: current system, who approves pay, who decides.
Asked for: a call this week.
Open questions: how crews are tracked and paid now, what has gone wrong, who else is involved.
Suggested opener: "You mentioned seasonal crews, walk me through a typical pay week."
Risk: none known. Do not promise outcomes.

## Rules
- No guaranteed meetings or outcomes. Do not promise what the call will deliver.
- Never book someone who did not show interest. Never call or text a person who has not asked.
- Voice comes after interest and agreement, never a cold call.
- Do not pressure with fake scarcity. Always make rescheduling easy.
- Use real calendar data or ask for it. Never invent availability.
- Drafts are drafts until the user approves.
- No dashes in any message.
